### 4a. Why the envelope criterion cannot be met as the targets are built

The criterion compares a gallery that is 100 % targeted anomalies against a
real population in which 24 of 672 windows carry a large deviation, so the
first suspicion is that the criterion is mis-specified. Per kind it still
fails, and the level-shift slice fails worst:

| population | n | exit | excess p95 | diff p99.9 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| real level shift | 6 | 0.167 | 3.90 | 0.774 |
| real ESA Point / Global | 18 | 0.167 | 3.93 | 0.861 |
| real ESA global subsequence | 636 | 0.031 | 4.23 | 0.073 |
| gen c1_repro, level shift | | 0.988 | 3.26 | 2.855 |
| gen contrast_masked, level shift | | **1.000** | 8.72 | 5.441 |
| gen contrast_masked, Point / Global | | 0.578 | 2.70 | 1.753 |
| gen contrast_masked, global subseq. | | 0.329 | 0.64 | 0.326 |

The planted sizes are right: `measure_contrast` on the generated level-shift
slice gives median 0.869 against the real 0.807, and its p90 is 3.987, which
is exactly the largest real value. The mechanism is the reference set.

* The six real level-shift amplitudes are **−0.865, −0.810, −0.804, −0.781,
  −0.742, +3.987** (channel-span units).
* The nominal donors sit a median of **0.033** above their channel floor and
  0.841 below the ceiling.

So `sample_targets` resamples a downward step of ≈ 0.8 into five sixths of the
generated level shifts, and a window whose parent has 0.033 of downward room
cannot absorb it — the exit is arithmetic, not a tuning error. The remaining
sixth draws +3.987 and produces the 3–9 unit overshoots behind excess p95 8.7.
Real level-shift windows exit only 1 in 6 times because the real events happen
where the signal has room; nothing in the objective knows that.

This is the n = 6 reference problem of
[NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md](NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md) L2/L6 in a second place:
a retrained denoiser cannot fix it, and neither can λ.

Three further findings.

**GenIAS is inert on this panel, under the published objective too.**
`anogen geniasfair` trains the paper's loss (learned ψ = 2.854, recon R²
0.982) and its gallery perturbs 2.7× more than the locked S3 one (RMSE to
donor 0.0023 vs 0.0008) — and still only **4.6 %** of its windows fall outside
the nominal set at q = 0.99, against 88–90 % for the steered recipes. Both
GenIAS arms *cost* an event against `real_only` (−0.027 [−0.055, +0.000]).
The "better than GenIAS" claim is therefore safe and nearly uninformative; the
honest sentence for the paper is that GenIAS at this window scale returns its
parent window.

**The novelty pass rate separates the arms better than ARP does.** Steering
moves it from 0.265 to 0.903 on the masked pair, where ΔARP against the same
twin is +0.237 with a 95 % interval that touches zero in standardized φ. With
the filter on (`results/shell_augdetect_filter/`), `contrast_only+real` is the
best plain arm at 0.727 and the GenIAS harm disappears (0.545 → 0.591 =
`real_only`), which identifies the harm as the nominal windows in its gallery.

**The headroom is where the checklist said it would be.** Generated positives
roughly double point-anomaly recall (0.078 → 0.167–0.178) and move level
shifts for C1 only (0.667 → 0.800; every other arm loses ground there). Global
subsequence recall is 0.149–0.154 for all twelve arms including `real_only` —
nothing any generator produced moves the kind that holds most of the events.

`real_frac: 0.5` (`results/shell_augdetect_realfrac/`) is worth one or two
events on several arms and nothing systematic, as in the testbed.

## 7. The retrain was not run (step 7)

`scripts/schedule_check.py --standardize` on the six channels: σ_data
0.69–0.93, and the largest σ_min keeping 95 % of the texture on **every**
channel is **0.050** (set by channel 2; the per-channel values are
0.050–0.079). The terminal-SNR bound is σ_max ≈ 109–162, so the
`sigma_max: 10.0` in the checklist's example block is about nine times too
small and would leave SNR_T near 9 %.

The retrain is not indicated by what step 4 found. Its levers are texture and
the from-noise level bias; the criteria that failed are envelope-exit
frequency and edge sharpness, and §4a traces both to the six level-shift
reference amplitudes and the donors' 0.033 of downward headroom. A new
checkpoint changes neither.

## 8. What to do next

1. **Make the contrast target respect the parent's headroom.** Choose the sign
   per window from the donor's room (0.033 down vs 0.841 up), or reject the
   sampled size when the parent cannot absorb it, instead of resampling the
   six amplitudes blind. This is the only change that can bring the envelope
   exit fraction near the budget, and it is confined to
   `shell/contrast.py::sample_targets` plus the plan in
   `phases/fixsweep.py::_contrast_plan`.
2. **Stop reporting the sixth amplitude as if it were a distribution.** One
   +3.987 window out of six becomes one sixth of the gallery and produces
   every excursion past 4 span units.
3. **Promote the novelty pass rate to a reported metric.** It separates
   steered from unsteered galleries (0.265 → 0.903) where ARP's interval
   touches zero, and it is what explains the GenIAS result.
4. **Point the paper's realism claim at `flip_x0_contrast_only`**, not at the
   masked variants: same position fix, a third of the sharpness defect, and
   the best fused detection arm (0.727, +0.182 [+0.045, +0.355] over
   `real_only`).
5. **Global subsequences remain untouched by every arm.** §8 of the checklist
   predicted this from the testbed; the ESA run confirms it at 0.15 recall
   across twelve arms. That is the generator work, not more steering terms.
