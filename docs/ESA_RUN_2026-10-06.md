### 4a. Why the envelope criterion cannot be met as the targets are built

The criterion compares a gallery that is 100 % targeted anomalies against a
real population in which 24 of 672 windows carry a large deviation, so the
first suspicion is that the criterion is mis-specified. Per kind it still
fails, and the level-shift slice fails worst:


| population                          | n   | exit      | excess p95 | diff p99.9 |
| ----------------------------------- | --- | --------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| real level shift                    | 6   | 0.167     | 3.90       | 0.774      |
| real ESA Point / Global             | 18  | 0.167     | 3.93       | 0.861      |
| real ESA global subsequence         | 636 | 0.031     | 4.23       | 0.073      |
| gen c1_repro, level shift           |     | 0.988     | 3.26       | 2.855      |
| gen contrast_masked, level shift    |     | **1.000** | 8.72       | 5.441      |
| gen contrast_masked, Point / Global |     | 0.578     | 2.70       | 1.753      |
| gen contrast_masked, global subseq. |     | 0.329     | 0.64       | 0.326      |


The planted sizes are right: `measure_contrast` on the generated level-shift
slice gives median 0.869 against the real 0.807, and its p90 is 3.987, which
is exactly the largest real value. The mechanism is the reference set.

- The six real level-shift amplitudes are **−0.865, −0.810, −0.804, −0.781,
−0.742, +3.987** (channel-span units).
- The nominal donors sit a median of **0.033** above their channel floor and
0.841 below the ceiling.

So `sample_targets` resamples a downward step of ≈ 0.8 into five sixths of the
generated level shifts, and a window whose parent has 0.033 of downward room
cannot absorb it — the exit is arithmetic, not a tuning error. The remaining
sixth draws +3.987 and produces the 3–9 unit overshoots behind excess p95 8.7.
Real level-shift windows exit only 1 in 6 times because the real events happen
where the signal has room; nothing in the objective knows that.

This is the n = 6 reference problem of
[NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md](NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md) L2/L6 in a second place:
a retrained denoiser cannot fix it, and neither can λ.

The defect is confined to the **step** target. The spike target is fine on
every variant (0.8–1.1×), and the two untargeted kinds are donor-smooth. On
the level-shift slice `flip_x0_contrast_only` regenerates the whole window at
ten times the real roughness, and `flip_x0_contrast_masked` keeps the donor
prefix exact — that part of the masking works — and returns a tail at twenty
times, with a spike at the transition reaching ±6 span units.

The mechanism follows from what the objective is. A step contrast is a
difference of means, so `contrast_edit` constrains the **mean** of the shifted
segment and says nothing about its variance; with `contrast_only` the
prototype term that would otherwise hold the texture is switched off by
construction. The sampler is free to satisfy the target with a noisy tail, and
does. The spike contrast is local (centre against flanks over ~23 bins) and
leaves no comparable freedom, which is why it survives.

So §4's reading of `flip_x0_contrast_only` as the better position fix holds
only for the position and the pooled tail statistic. On the kind it targets it
is not usable as it stands, and neither are the masked variants; C1 and
`flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1` are the only variants whose level shifts have
the right texture, and they put the step at the start of the window.

## 8. What to do next

1. **Make the contrast target respect the parent's headroom.** Choose the sign
  per window from the donor's room (0.033 down vs 0.841 up), or reject the
   sampled size when the parent cannot absorb it, instead of resampling the
   six amplitudes blind. This is the only change that can bring the envelope
   exit fraction near the budget, and it is confined to
   `shell/contrast.py::sample_targets` plus the plan in
   `phases/fixsweep.py::_contrast_plan`.
2. **Stop reporting the sixth amplitude as if it were a distribution.** One
  +3.987 window out of six becomes one sixth of the gallery and produces
   every excursion past 4 span units.
3. **Promote the novelty pass rate to a reported metric.** It separates
  steered from unsteered galleries (0.265 → 0.903) where ARP's interval
   touches zero, and it is what explains the GenIAS result.
4. **Constrain the variance of a shifted segment, not only its mean.** §4b is
  the reason no contrast variant carried a realism claim at the time of
   writing: the step target fixes the mean of the tail and leaves its texture
   free, at 10–22× the real roughness. **Done, by a different route than the
   one guessed here — see §9.** Neither the variance contrast nor the surviving
   prototype term was needed; the roughness was a symptom of planting the shift
   on the wrong side of the window.
5. **Global subsequences remain untouched by every arm.** §8 of the checklist
  predicted this from the testbed; the ESA run confirms it at 0.15 recall
   across twelve arms. That is the generator work, not more steering terms.

## 9. Closing the realism gap on the level shift

§4b ended with the level-shift slice at 10–22× the real texture roughness and
the recommendation to constrain its variance. That diagnosis was right about
the symptom and wrong about the cause. Six measurements, each refuting the
previous fix, locate it.

### 9.1 What a real level shift looks like, and what we were making

Split each window at its best difference-of-means point and measure the mean
|first difference| inside the shifted side, separately for the segment's edges
(outer 10% each side) and its interior:


|                                                 | edge   | interior | edge / interior |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------ | -------- | --------------- |
| real level shift                                | 0.1770 | 0.0117   | 15.1            |
| `nosteer_flip_x0_masked` (donor reconstruction) | 0.0091 | 0.0099   | 0.9             |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked`                       | 0.6440 | 0.4759   | 1.4             |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room`                  | 0.3477 | 0.2031   | 1.7             |


A real level shift is **flat, jump, flat**: all of its variation is the jump.
What the generator made was a segment with the right mean and a *noisy
interior*, 17–40× the real one. The donor reconstruction is as smooth as real
(0.0099 against 0.0117), so the texture is not the denoiser's doing — the
guidance injects it. [kind_grid_best/real_level_shift.png](kind_grid_best/real_level_shift.png)
shows eight draws per recipe against all six real windows; the nearest-in-φ
galleries of §4b hide this, because they show one hand-picked draw per cell.

### 9.2 Why: the direction was inverted, and the parent could not be fixed

Every real level shift drops **from the top of the channel span to the bottom**
(pre-level 0.835–0.920, post-level 0.073–0.131, the one exception being the
ch-2 window at −3.904). The nominal donors sit at the bottom: median level
0.099, p95 0.123. So `fit_to_headroom` (§4a) was doing its job correctly and
flipping 86.5% of the sampled amplitudes upward, which is the *time reverse* of
the real morphology, into a level the channel-conditioned score has never seen.
The score then opposes the projection at every one of the 50 steps, and the
residue of that fight is the noisy interior.

Giving the slice a parent that can hold the drop does not work on this panel.
Counting nominal donors by level:


|                                                    | donors | level > 0.25 | > 0.6 | > 0.8 |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------------ | ----- | ----- |
| full nominal pool, per channel                     | 4096   | 4–11         | 1–4   | 0–4   |
| the S3 donor pool fixsweep draws from, per channel | 256    | 0–1          | 0–1   | 0–1   |


Eleven windows in the entire pool sit above 0.8, against the 64 per channel the
slice needs. The high state of these channels is not nominal behaviour at all:
it occurs only as the pre-drop state of the six labelled events. `contrast_donor_room`
(`shell/contrast.py::assign_donors_by_room`) is implemented and tested, and it
changes nothing here for exactly that reason — `flip_x0_contrast_parent` is
within noise of `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room` on every metric.

### 9.3 The fix: shift the head, not the tail

The step contrast is a difference of means, and `contrast_mask` assumes the
shifted side is the **tail** (`[p − dilate, end)`). A real level shift shifts
the **head**: it is high for the first ~0.3 h of the 4.27 h window and then
drops for good — every real split sits at 0.072 W. So `"step_head"` mirrors
the weights and the mask, and then the five positive reference amplitudes lift
a short early stretch off a low donor. That needs 0.8 of *upward* room against
the 0.9 the donor has, so nothing has to be flipped and nothing leaves the
conditional support; and the mask locks the whole tail to the donor, which is
why the tail comes out as clean as the donor reconstruction.

Two further knobs finish it. `contrast_pos` keeps the split near where the real
ones are (the frozen W/16 guard puts the reachable range at 0.0625–0.18 W).
`contrast_polish` runs an SDEdit pass (Meng et al., ICLR 2022) **on the level
shift only** — re-noise to ν = 0.35 and let the unguided denoiser walk back
down, then re-apply the projection once so the amplitude the polish removed
comes back without the chain getting another 50 steps to roughen it. Applying
it to the spike as well halves a 3-bin excursion (0.830 → 0.303), which is why
it is per-kind.

Each step measured on the level-shift slice, as a multiple of the real
roughness, with the Point/Global spike height beside it:


| recipe                                                            | level-shift roughness  | spike height | morphology           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------ | -------------------- |
| real                                                              | 1.0× (0.0160 span/bin) | 0.830        | high, drop, flat     |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked`                                         | 21.8×                  | 1.040        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room` (headroom)                         | 5.8×                   | 1.040        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room_decay2` (contrast decay)            | 5.0×                   | 1.018        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head` (head)                                    | 5.5×                   | 1.040        | **high, drop, flat** |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head_polish3` (head + polish both kinds)        | **1.5×**               | 0.303        | high, drop, flat     |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head_kindpolish` (head + polish the shift only) | **1.5×**               | **1.040**    | **high, drop, flat** |


`contrast_decay` and `polish_reproject` are in `shell/steer.py` as opt-in
switches with 0 / False defaults; `test_defaults_reproduce_legacy` still passes
bit-for-bit.

## 10 Randomising the shifted side, and learning the contrast weights

Two questions were open after §9: whether the level shift has to sit on the
same end of every window, and whether the hand-written `w[t]` is the right
template or just the first one I tried. Both are now answered, and the second
one found a defect worth fixing.

### 10.1 `contrast_mirror`: which end carries the shift

`mirror_rows` time-reverses the contrast and its mask for a random share of
the windows. Reversing `w` turns `step_head` at split *p* into its own mirror,
a `step` at split *W − p*, with the amplitude sign unchanged — so the shifted
side becomes the tail without touching the amplitude distribution or the
headroom fit. `contrast_mirror: {kind: frac}` sets the share; the default is
`None`, i.e. the frozen behaviour.

At `frac = 0.5` the splits land 50.1% head / 49.9% tail, against 100% head
before, and **nothing else moves**: level-shift roughness 1.5× real, spike
height 1.040, envelope exit 0.556, ARP 0.496 [0.256, 0.764] — all identical to
`flip_x0_contrast_head_kindpolish` to three decimals. The side was free.

This is a different knob from `contrast_pos`, which sets *how far in* the
split is. §9.5's pin is still there; the mirror removes only the left/right
asymmetry, which is the part a detector could trivially learn.

### 10.2 Can `w` be learned?

`w` is a template placed at a sampled position, so the learnable object is the
template, not a whole-window vector — a window-length vector fitted on the
real anomalies would bake their positions in and undo the position fix. Three
standard learned detectors were fitted to the real patches and compared to the
hand-written template, leave-one-event-out (`/tmp/learnw.py`):

| | matched filter | whitened matched filter (Σ⁻¹μ) | logistic regression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point/Global, detection margin | 8.76 | **8.93** | 8.84 |
| cos to the hand template | +0.533 | +0.527 | +0.530 |

against the hand template's **2.88**. AUC is 1.0000 for all four, which is why
the margin (median anomaly score / p99.9 nominal score) is the comparison:
these anomalies are trivially separable and AUC cannot rank the templates.

The level shift has 6 windows from a single event, so no held-out split
exists; reported in-sample, the learned template recovers the hand-written
step exactly (|cos| = 1.000, margin 5.18 for both). **The hand step is the
learned optimum** and nothing is left on the table there.

### 10.3 What the Point/Global gap actually was

The learned templates all concentrate on one bin
([template_candidates_v2.png](template_candidates_v2.png)), and the raw data
agrees: **all 18 real Point/Global excursions are exactly 1 bin wide at half
maximum** ([real_spikes_zoom.png](real_spikes_zoom.png)). The hand template
averaged a 3-bin centre (`spike_half=1`), which dilutes the measured amplitude
threefold — reference amplitudes read 0.274 with a 3-bin centre against 0.808
with a 1-bin one, where the real excursion height is 0.831 — and plants a
3-bin-wide spike.

`contrast_geom: {kind: {...}}` passes the shape parameters through to
`measure_contrast`, `contrast_weights` and `contrast_mask` together, so the
amplitudes and the planted contrast stay in the same units. Default `{}`,
i.e. the frozen geometry.

`flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp` is `..._mirror` plus `spike_half: 0`:

| | width (bins, median) | share exactly 1 bin | height | ARP | Cov | Div | ΔARP vs twin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real | 1 | 100% | 0.831 | — | — | — | — |
| `..._head_sharp` | **1** | **53%** | **0.988** | 0.499 [0.258, 0.766] | **0.033** | **9.68** | **+0.177 [+0.018, +0.330]** |
| `..._head_mirror` | 3 | 27% | 1.040 | 0.496 [0.256, 0.764] | 0.021 | 8.66 | +0.174 [+0.015, +0.331] |
| `c1_repro` | 1 | 53% | 0.447 | 0.567 [0.373, 0.764] | 0.015 | 7.30 | — |

ARP is unchanged, coverage and diversity both rise, and the morphology now
matches. C2ST moves 0.868 → 0.881, within the fold spread. The difference is
visible without measuring it in
[kind_grid_best/real_ESA_Point_-_Global.png](kind_grid_best/real_ESA_Point_-_Global.png):
`sharp` draws hairlines like the real ones, `mirror` draws bars.

`flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp` replaces `flip_x0_contrast_head_kindpolish` as
the recommendation. Everything in §9.5 still applies except the left/right
asymmetry, which §10.1 removed.

### 10.4 Still open

- C1's spikes are *also* 1 bin wide (53%) but half the real height (0.447) and
  sit on a baseline at 1.5× the real roughness, so the width agreement there
  is not evidence that C1 has the morphology right.
- The two kinds without a contrast functional (`real ESA global subsequence`,
  `real ESA local subsequence`) still run on prototype guidance, untouched by
  §9–§10. They are the obvious next target: a functional for them would make
  the recipe's advantage apply to all four kinds rather than two.
- §9.5's envelope exit (0.555) is unchanged.

### 10.5 Detector utility: the recipe does not improve detection

`shell.augdetect.arms` gained `fixsweep:flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp+real`
(its no-steer twin was already listed, so the pair is complete) and the run
was repeated; the pre-§10 results are kept at
`results/shell_augdetect_pre_head/`. Event recall at 1% nominal FAR, dev only:

| arm | event recall | noleak | AP | rare FAR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `real_only` | 0.591 | 0.591 | 0.472 | 0.026 |
| `c1_plus_real` | 0.636 | 0.714 | 0.479 | 0.051 |
| `flip_x0_contrast_only+real` | 0.636 | 0.714 | **0.483** | 0.059 |
| `flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1+real` | **0.727** | 0.714 | 0.472 | 0.088 |
| **`flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp+real`** | 0.545 | 0.643 | 0.470 | 0.036 |
| `nosteer_flip_x0_masked+real` | 0.545 | 0.571 | 0.468 | 0.024 |

Against `real_only` the paired gain is +0.018 [−0.100, +0.136], p = 0.41 —
no gain. Against `c1_plus_real` it is −0.064 [−0.136, −0.009] — significantly
worse. Only against its own no-steer twin is it positive, and only fused with
the diffusion score: +0.036 [+0.000, +0.082], p = 0.04.

So the §4b recommendation and the §10 recommendation are for *different
questions*. `flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1` remains the arm to cite for
detector utility — at +0.145 [−0.027, +0.327], p = 0.053 against `real_only`,
itself only marginal — and `flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp` is the one to cite
for morphology and controllability, with **no detection claim attached**.

#### Why the two leaders differ: the edit-footprint hypothesis

The steering's edit can be read off exactly, because every variant has a
no-steer twin run with the same chain, seeds and masks: `x − x_twin` *is* the
edit. Share of bins moved by more than 0.05 in channel-span units:

| | whole gallery | Point/Global | level shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp` | 20.6% | **0.9%** | 14.0% |
| `flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1` | 49.3% | **69.2%** | 68.9% |

A generated Point/Global from the recommended recipe differs from a plain
nominal donor in about **five bins out of 512**. That is the whole point of
the masked contrast design — it is why the morphology is right and why the
window comes with a bin-level label — but to a window-level detector such a
window is nearly a nominal one.

**Hypothesis: detector utility tracks how much of the window the steering
changes, not how faithfully the result matches the real morphology.** The
supporting observation is that `flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp+real` scores
0.545 event recall, *exactly* its no-steer twin's 0.545 — and the twin
contains no anomaly at all. `flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1`, which rewrites
two thirds of every window, beats its own twin by +0.191 [−0.018, +0.400].

This is a hypothesis, not a result. Four things would test it, and the first
two can be run on the existing stack:

1. Widen the contrast support at fixed morphology (`step_span`, a longer
   masked segment, functionals for the two subsequence kinds). If the
   hypothesis holds, detection rises with footprint while the morphology
   diagnostics stay put.
2. Drop `contrast_only`, so the prototype term deforms the rest of the window
   as well. Detection should rise and C2ST should get worse.
3. Train on the union of the two galleries; if the mechanisms are
   complementary rather than competing, the union beats both.
4. Score **bin-level** localization rather than event recall. The contrast
   recipes are the only ones that emit a bin-level label, and if the
   hypothesis is right they should *win* that endpoint while losing this one.
   This is the measurement most likely to change the recommendation, and it
   is not implemented.


## 11 The budget sweep, and a generator that does help detection

§10.5 concluded that the recommended recipe does not improve detection. That
conclusion was measured at the wrong operating point, and it is wrong.

### 11.1 What `augdetect` was measuring

Every `+real` arm trains on **all** ~450 real training anomalies plus a fixed
256 generated ones:

```python
n_synth = int(acfg.get("n_synth", 256))
x_pos_real = x_a[train_a] if use_real else ...   # all of them
```

The real positives outnumber the generated ones about 2:1, so the detector is
nowhere near the few-shot regime a generator exists to serve. The frozen arms
report a single point on a curve, at the end where the curve is flat.

`shell.augdetect.sweep` (opt-in, absent from the config nothing changes) adds
the curve: for each k the detector trains on **k real anomalies plus k
generated ones**, against a `real_only` reference at the same k. All arms at a
given (k, fold, seed) see the *same* k real windows, so the comparison is
paired. `sweep.only: true` leaves the frozen arms untouched; results land in
`sweep.json`, `sweep.csv` and [augdetect/sweep.png](augdetect/sweep.png).

### 11.2 Augmentation does help, in the regime that matters

Paired event-recall gain over `real_only` at the same k, 3 folds × 5 seeds,
bootstrap over events; **bold** = 95% CI excludes zero.

| k | `c1_plus_real` | `final025_recur2_decay1` | `..._head_sharp` | `..._head_sharp_nomask` |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0.091 | +0.036 | −0.009 | +0.009 |
| 2 | **+0.136** | −0.018 | +0.009 | −0.009 |
| 4 | **+0.182** | +0.082 | +0.055 | +0.027 |
| 8 | **+0.182** | **+0.100** | +0.091 | **+0.155** |
| 16 | **+0.200** | +0.118 | +0.036 | +0.164 |
| 32 | **+0.236** | **+0.164** | **+0.109** | **+0.200** |
| 64 | **+0.127** | +0.100 | −0.027 | **+0.136** |
| 128 | +0.091 | +0.127 | −0.018 | +0.118 |
| 256 | +0.082 | +0.118 | +0.045 | +0.109 |

The gain peaks around k = 16–32 and fades by k = 128–256, which is where the
frozen arms sit. `nosteer_flip_x0_masked+real`, whose windows contain no
anomaly, is negative at k = 2, 4, 64 and 256 — the negative control behaves.

`c1_plus_real` is strongest at small k, and not because of its fold-0
prototype leak: with the leaked fold dropped it is *higher* still (0.714 from
k = 16 on against 0.636 pooled).

### 11.3 The edit-footprint hypothesis, tested

§10.5 predicted that raising how much of the window the steering changes
would buy detection. Three variants raise it without touching the contrast
morphology; the prediction was specific enough to be wrong.

| | footprint, all / P/G | gain at k=32 | ARP | Cov | Div | spike width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `..._head_sharp` | 20.6% / 0.9% | **+0.109** | 0.499 | 0.033 | 9.68 | 1 bin, 53% |
| `..._sharp_proto` (keep the prototype term) | 20.6% / 0.9% | +0.164 | 0.549 | 0.027 | 10.55 | 2 bins, 44% |
| `..._sharp_dilate64` (8× the mask margin) | 22.7% / 0.9% | — | 0.498 | 0.032 | 9.69 | 1 bin, 53% |
| `..._sharp_nomask` (no RePaint mask) | **42.1% / 28.7%** | **+0.200** | 0.509 | **0.038** | 10.53 | **1 bin, 99%** |
| `..._sharp_nomask_proto` (both) | — | +0.182 | — | — | — | — |

The RePaint mask, not `contrast_only`, is what pins the footprint: keeping
the prototype term changes nothing (20.6% either way) because everything
outside the mask is overwritten with the donor regardless. Dropping the mask
triples the footprint and moves detection from +0.109 to +0.200 at k = 32,
significant at k = 8, 32 and 64. Doing both at once adds nothing further.

### 11.4 Dropping the mask also improves realism

This was not predicted and is the more interesting half of the result.

| | real | `..._head_sharp` | `..._sharp_nomask` |
|---|---|---|---|
| spike height | 0.831 | 0.988 | **0.838** |
| share exactly 1 bin | 100% | 53% | **99%** |
| level-shift roughness | 1.00× | 1.53× | **1.21×** |
| baseline texture | 1.00× | 0.97× | 0.83× |
| noise-burst draws | 0.0% | 0.0% | 3.0% |
| draws with ≥3 deep downward dips | 0.0% | 2.0% | 14.8% |
| ARP | — | 0.499 | **0.509** |
| Coverage@τ | — | 0.033 | **0.038** |
| Div | — | 9.68 | **10.53** |
| ΔARP vs twin | — | +0.177 [+0.018, +0.330] | **+0.191 [+0.039, +0.346]** |

The mask was *causing* the texture defect: it forces a hard seam between the
edited span and the untouched donor, and the guided chain has to resolve the
discontinuity inside the masked region. Without it the chain is consistent
across the whole window, so the planted spike lands at its real height and
width and the level-shift transition stops being ragged. The price is the
bin-level label (there is no mask to export) and a visible artifact: 14.8% of
Point/Global draws carry three or more deep downward dips in the baseline,
against 2.0% masked and 0.0% real. The mask had been suppressing them by
holding the baseline at the donor. C1 has 48.4%, so this is still far better
than the baseline recipe, but it is the clearest remaining defect and it is
what a reviewer will see first.

### 11.5 Recommendation

**`flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp_nomask`** replaces `..._head_sharp`. It is the
better model on the frozen protocol, on every morphology diagnostic except
noise-burst rate, and it is the first recipe in this document with a
detection gain that is both significant and attached to the recommended
recipe: **+0.200 [+0.072, +0.336] at k = 32**, +0.136 at k = 64, matching or
beating `c1_plus_real` from k = 64 up.

Keep `flip_x0_contrast_head_sharp` when the bin-level label is needed; it is
the only recipe that emits one.

### 11.6 Still open

- `c1_plus_real` remains ahead at k ≤ 32 despite 22.8% noise-burst draws and a
  collapsed spike position. Nothing in the footprint story explains why a
  *worse-looking* generator wins the smallest budgets, and the §10.5 reading
  (variety near the nominal manifold) is still untested.
- The sweep subsamples real positives uniformly over windows, so small k is
  dominated by the two subsequence kinds (648 of 672 windows). A kind-
  stratified sweep would say something different and is not implemented.
- The bin-level localization endpoint from §10.5 is still not implemented,
  and it is now the measurement that would decide between the two recipes.
