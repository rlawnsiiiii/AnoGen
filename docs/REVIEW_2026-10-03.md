# Critical review of the current results (03.10.2026)

Scope: everything in `docs/` up to [NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md](NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md)
and the abstract (`paper/abstract.tex`), read against the code. No new ESA
numbers are produced here. New *measurements* need the workstation, and the
commands are in §5. Mechanism evidence comes from the torch-free testbed
([TESTBED.md](TESTBED.md)). Fixes are in [POSITION_BIAS.md](POSITION_BIAS.md).

## 0. Bottom line

The engineering and the bookkeeping are strong: frozen τ and φ, a sealed
test, event-OOF references, isolated result directories, and leaks flagged
rather than hidden. The generator works and is diverse. However, the two
headline claims of the abstract ("comparable or superior realism" and
"nearly doubles diversity") currently rest on comparisons that cannot carry
them, and the third ("type-targeted") is not supported for the one type
that needs it most.

1. **ARP does not isolate anomaly realism.** Three galleries with no
   anomaly information at all span most of the ARP range in the project:

   | gallery | anomaly information | ARP |
   |---|---|---:|
   | real nominal donors (= `genias_patched`, bit-identical) | none | 0.320 |
   | DDIM edit of the donors, no steering, ν = 0.2 | none | 0.451–0.470 |
   | DDIM edit of the donors, no steering, ν = 1 | none | 0.568 |
   | **C1 chanmix** (the reportable C1) | prototypes from train-fold faults | **0.565** |

   ARP rises with *how much of a nominal window the sampler regenerates*,
   before any anomaly term is switched on. C1 does not beat unguided DDIM.
   GenIAS scores 0.317 because, as implemented here, it is a near-copy of the
   donor (RMSE 8e-4; §2.3), not because it is a bad anomaly generator. So
   "superior realism vs GenIAS" currently means "DDIM-regenerated telemetry
   sits closer to real anomalies in φ than real nominal telemetry does".
   That is a statement about φ and the sampler, and an interesting one, but
   it is not anomaly realism.
2. **"Nearly doubles diversity" compares against near-copies of real
   nominal data.** GenIAS and post-hoc inherit the donor set's spread, so
   their EDI is essentially the EDI of real nominal telemetry. Spread beyond
   the real anomaly distribution is not a virtue in itself. That needs a
   fidelity-aware measure (precision/recall, density/coverage), which is now
   implemented (§4).
3. **Realism by every raw-window test is the weak point.** The linear C2ST
   rejects C1 in 14/15 runs (0.795). Envelope exits happen 15× too often,
   edges are 2.8× too sharp, and 47 % of dominant peaks sit in the first
   tenth of the window (real 11 %). The root causes are now identified, see
   §3.
4. **Downstream detection shows no significant gain** (14/22 vs 13/22
   events), and the experiment had a train/test overlap that is now fixed
   (§2.6).

None of this sinks the paper. It changes what the paper should claim and
which rows every table needs (§6).

## 2. Issues with the evidence

### 2.1 ARP mixes texture, spread and anomaly shape

φ = `feature_pack_v1` z-scores each window. What is left is the shape of the
z-path, the first-difference moments, peak |z|, excursion length and four
spectral band energies. DDIM regeneration changes exactly these: it alters
high-frequency texture (in the testbed, 20-step DDIM produces first
differences at 0.6× the real spread) and it scatters windows more widely
than the donors. ARP is a mean nearest-neighbour distance from each real
query to the gallery, so a gallery that is more spread *and* shifted in
texture toward where real anomaly windows sit wins without containing
anomalies. The monotone ordering donor < ν=0.2 no-steer < ν=1 unguided ≈
C1 is that effect.

There is a concrete candidate mechanism (testbed E7). φ z-scores every
window, so two of its 12 components are constants, and it is not scaled
across features. Among nominal windows, the three upper log-FFT band
energies carry most of the pairwise distance (78 % in the testbed). Those
are exactly the components that DDIM regeneration shifts (by 1–1.6
nominal sd, with no anomaly content). They are also the components that
fall when a window's z-scoring is dominated by a large low-frequency
excursion, as in most anomalies. `scripts/audit_metrics.py` reports the φ
attribution and an ARP in per-feature standardized φ (constants dropped) as
a sensitivity column. If the ranking flips there, the frozen-φ ARP claim
needs that caveat in the paper.

What to report instead:

- every ARP table carries the **donor** row (0.320) and the **unguided** row
  (0.568), and C1's paired ARP difference against each with a CI. Before
  claiming anomaly-specific realism, the difference vs unguided must be
  positive;
- an **oracle** row: real anomaly windows from the *other* folds used as
  the gallery. This is what reproducing known faults exactly would score,
  and it is the honest ceiling;
- **precision / density** (fidelity) and **recall / coverage** (spread)
  against the real anomaly set (Naeem et al. 2020), from the same φ;
- the **φ attribution** of the query→nearest distance, to see which
  feature carries the gains (e.g. a high-frequency band instead of the
  excursion features).

All of these are in `scripts/audit_metrics.py` (numpy, minutes, saved
artifacts only).

### 2.2 Coverage@τ was set aside after it disagreed

Coverage@τ was the pre-registered S0 win rule. On it, every steered variant
loses by an order of magnitude (C1 chanmix 0.022 vs post-hoc 0.267). The
paper switched to GenIAS's ARP/EDI. That is defensible, since it is the
baseline paper's own protocol, but a reviewer who sees the repo will notice
the switch. Report Coverage@τ too, with its CI, and explain the
disagreement. τ is a 10 % quantile of unguided distances on fold 0, a small
ball, so Coverage measures "close copies of real faults" while ARP measures
average closeness.

### 2.3 The GenIAS baseline is not GenIAS

The paper's GenIAS trains with a learned ψ, a triplet-margin perturbation
loss (δ_min = 0.1, δ_max = 0.2, β = 0.1) and a compact KL with σ_prior = 0.5
(arXiv:2502.08262, Eq. 2–6). The S3 model trains reconstruction + KL only and
fixes ψ = 2. Its samples are therefore 8e-4 RMSE from the donor, and the
Alg. 2 patch never fires, so `genias_patched` is the donor bit for bit.
Algorithm 2 also works per *dimension* on the whole trajectory, not per
timestep. Any reviewer who knows GenIAS will reject the comparison as it
stands.

Done: `train_genias(faithful=True)` implements the published objective,
including Eq. 2 as printed, which is not the textbook KL (`kl_form` switches).
`genias_patch_alg2` implements Algorithm 2, and the `geniasfair` phase trains,
samples and scores it in an isolated directory. Alg. 2's criterion compares
units² with units, so τ is reported on a grid in raw and whitened units
instead of transferring the paper's value.

### 2.4 No uncertainty, and the effective sample size is small

There are 672 anomaly windows but only 22 events. One event contributes up
to 8 crops per channel, and global subsequence is 636 windows from 17
events. Window-level means treat these as independent. With 3 folds and
fold 2 holding 126 windows, differences such as 0.584 vs 0.565 (contiguous
vs chanmix) or 0.576 vs 0.584 (C2 vs C1) cannot be called either way
today. Done: event-cluster bootstrap CIs, optionally also resampling the
gallery, and paired differences on shared queries (`shell/evaluation.py`).
These are wired into `fixsweep` and `audit_metrics.py`.

### 2.5 The linear C2ST is a weak test that C1 still fails

The real *nominal* donors are indistinguishable from real *anomaly* windows
for the frozen linear C2ST (0.509). So passing it means "plausible
telemetry", not "plausible anomaly". The injection baselines pass because
they are mostly donor. C1 fails even this weak test, which makes the
failure informative and a pass uninformative. Keep it as a floor, and pair
it with envelope frequency, edge sharpness and the peak-position
distribution.

### 2.6 augdetect: underpowered, and the train and test sets overlapped

- **Overlap (now fixed).** Test nominals for fold k are the S3 donors with
  `index % 3 == k`. Every synthetic gallery is stored in donor order (row i
  edits donor i), and synthetic positives were drawn from all 1536 rows.
  So a fold-k test negative's own edited copy could be a training positive.
  The direction of the bias is unknown, since it can help or hurt depending
  on how much of the donor survives the edit, but the experiment was not
  clean. `donor_disjoint: true` (new default) draws positives only from
  donors outside the test fold. `donor_disjoint: false` reproduces the old
  table.
- **Power.** The primary endpoint is event recall at 1 % FAR on 22 events.
  The FAR threshold is set by the top ~5 of 512 test nominals. A one-event
  difference is 4.5 points. Done: a paired event bootstrap of each arm vs
  `real_only` (`vs_real_only` in the summary). Also report AP/AUROC, which
  use every window, with CIs.
- **Position shortcut (P7).** Done: every run also scores the time-flipped
  test set (`event_recall_flipped`). A detector that learned "anomaly =
  window start" loses recall there. `shift_aug` adds circular-shift training
  augmentation. The CNN ends in global average pooling, so the risk is
  smaller than for a dense head, but it has to be measured, not assumed.

### 2.7 Type targeting rests on very little data

There are 6 level-shift windows, all crops of `id_83` and all in fold 0,
so folds 1–2 use them as OOF references and fold 0 leaks. There are 12
local-subsequence windows from 2 events, and 18 Point/Global windows from
3 events. The per-kind tables are descriptive. State this in the paper
instead of a type-targeting claim that implies generalization.

### 2.8 "From noise" is off the training distribution

The linear schedule ends at ᾱ_T = 0.132, so q(x_T | x₀) keeps 0.36 × x₀. The
denoiser has never seen a zero-mean input at t = T−1. `start_from_noise`
feeds it N(0, I) all the same. In the testbed this biases the generated
level by −0.2 to −0.3 nominal standard deviations, which φ cannot see.
The same fact means "unguided ν = 1" keeps 36 % of the donor's amplitude
and is still a donor edit. Done: `noise_init_mean/std` (fixsweep
`matched_noise: true`) starts from the marginal of q(x_T). The cleaner fix
is a zero-terminal-SNR schedule (Lin et al. 2024), which needs a retrain.

## 3. Root causes of the realism failures (summary of POSITION_BIAS.md)

1. **The S4-D backbone is causal**: ε_θ(x_t)[i] sees only x_t[0..i]. Even the
   best possible causal estimator puts 41–61 % of unguided peaks in the first
   tenth of the window and ~1 % in the last tenth. This explains L2 (bias
   with no steering at all) and predicts something the repo has not
   measured: C1 and unguided should have almost no peaks in the *last*
   tenth. `scripts/diagnose_causality.py` checks this in minutes.
2. **x-space guidance goes through that causal Jacobian.** An edit aimed at
   the window centre lands at 25 % of the window, and unit normalization
   hands the early bins the whole step. Likely cause of L1, the
   level-shift transient.
3. **Constant-norm kicks, and the last one is not denoised.** This drives
   edge sharpness and envelope frequency (L3, L4). hashfix proved the
   binary version.
4. **Six prototypes for 384 samples with a temporal encoder** fix the event
   position (P2.1).
5. **The noise schedule erases fine texture.** The linear β schedule has σ_min
   = 0.01 in scaled units and only a few steps in the low-noise range. Even an
   *exact* denoiser then reproduces only 36–68 % of a telemetry model's
   first-difference variance (testbed E10). ESA unguided output has 0.71× the
   donors' first-difference p99.9. The fix is a log-spaced schedule, retrained as
   `anogen s1v2` together with the bidirectional backbone.

## 5. Order of work on the workstation

1. `pytest -q` (the torch tests must pass, especially the legacy
   equivalence).
2. `scripts/diagnose_causality.py`: confirms or kills the causal diagnosis
   in five minutes.
3. `scripts/audit_metrics.py`: re-judges every saved gallery with CIs,
   donor/unguided/oracle rows and PRDC. This alone changes how the abstract
   should be worded.
4. `anogen fixsweep`: the zero-retrain fixes. The decision rule is declared
   in POSITION_BIAS.md §7.
5. `anogen s1bidir` and `anogen s1v2`, then add the `bidir_x0` / `v2_x0`
   variants and rerun `fixsweep` (POSITION_BIAS.md §6).
6. `anogen geniasfair`, then `anogen augdetect` (leak-free).
7. `anogen diffdetect`, a label-free detector from the S1 score. Its nominal
   negatives are fresh panel windows that S1 never trained on, because every
   donor and every labelled rare is in S1's training pool. Then set
   `augdetect.fuse_diffscore` and rerun augdetect for the fused
   CNN + diffusion detector. It is compared to the CNN alone on the same
   held-out negatives, with a paired event bootstrap.

## 6. Suggested framing until the new numbers exist

- Realism: "higher ARP than GenIAS and post-hoc injection, at the level of
  unguided diffusion", with the donor and unguided rows in the table.
- Diversity: report PRDC recall/coverage next to EDI, and the EDI union
  explicitly.
- Type targeting: "steers toward a chosen prototype; demonstrated for
  Point/Global spikes; level shifts rest on a single labelled event".
- From noise: keep the claim, add the envelope/level diagnostics, and use
  the matched start.
- Detection: "comparable to training on real anomalies when used as the
  only positives; no significant gain when added", until the leak-free
  rerun says otherwise.
