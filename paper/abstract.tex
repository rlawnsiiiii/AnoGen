\title{GenFSDiff: Few-shot Steered Diffusion for Generation of Synthetic Spacecraft Anomalies}

Satellite telemetry anomaly detection is essential for scalable spacecraft operations but remains challenging due to the scarcity of confirmed anomalies in public datasets like the ESA Anomaly Detection Benchmark (ESA-ADB). This motivates the generation of synthetic anomalies to enable more realistic telemetry simulations and improve time series anomaly detection (TSAD) accuracy. However, existing generation methods mostly rely on ad-hoc, hand-crafted strategies applied to raw time series. These approaches often fail to capture diverse, complex anomalous patterns and cannot generate anomalies from scratch (e.g., from pure noise). We propose a novel synthetic anomaly generation method named \underline{Gen}erator for Anomalies in Satellite Telemetry using \underline{F}ew-shot \underline{S}teered \underline{Diff}usion \textbf{(GenFSDiff)}. 

GenFSDiff is trained on nominal and rare-nominal windows of six ESA-ADB Mission 1 channels (channels 41–46). At sample time, it can be \textit{steered} to produce realistic, diverse, univariate anomalies of specific types, inspired by the ESA-ADB categorization. To achieve this, a real nominal parent time series is noised to a moderate diffusion time and steered using an energy-based force: 
\[
  f(\hat{x})=\lambda_{\mathrm{shell}}(h_{\mathrm{nom}}(e(\hat{x}))-Q_{q})^2
    +\lambda_{\mathrm{anom}}h_{\mathrm{anom}}(e(\hat{x}))
\]
Here, $\hat{x}$ is the anomalous window being denoised/generated at sample time. The term $h_{\mathrm{nom}}$ represents soft energies of $\hat{x}$ against nominals in a frozen encoder $e(\cdot)$ that is trained with reconstruction and an auxiliary supervised-contrastive loss on train-fold labels, i.e., including known rare-nominals and anomalies (hence, "few-shot"). $Q_q$ represents the $q$th-quantile of nominal energy (e.g. $Q_{0.99}$ bounds the sample to the rarest 1\% of nominals in encoder space). To target a specific anomaly type, $h_{\mathrm{anom}}$ is set to the squared error between the embeddings of $\hat{x}$ and a specific known sample of the wanted anomaly type in the encoder space. Ultimately, this steering process guides the denoising trajectory toward a specific anomaly type while penalizing large deviations from a defined $Q_{0.99}$-shell of nominals.

We evaluate GenFSDiff against a basic hand-crafted baseline and GenIAS, a recently published variational autoencoder-based model. Following the GenIAS methodology, we measure generation quality using Anomalous Representation Proximity (ARP) for realism and the Entropy-Based Diversity Index (EDI) for diversity. Our results demonstrate that GenFSDiff achieves comparable or superior realism, nearly doubles the diversity score, and introduces a unique capability to target specific anomaly types. Furthermore, unlike existing methods, our trained model does not require raw time series as parents; it can generate anomalies from pure noise without significantly degrading its ARP and EDI scores.
