### 4a. Why the envelope criterion cannot be met as the targets are built

The criterion compares a gallery that is 100 % targeted anomalies against a
real population in which 24 of 672 windows carry a large deviation, so the
first suspicion is that the criterion is mis-specified. Per kind it still
fails, and the level-shift slice fails worst:


| population                          | n   | exit      | excess p95 | diff p99.9 |
| ----------------------------------- | --- | --------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| real level shift                    | 6   | 0.167     | 3.90       | 0.774      |
| real ESA Point / Global             | 18  | 0.167     | 3.93       | 0.861      |
| real ESA global subsequence         | 636 | 0.031     | 4.23       | 0.073      |
| gen c1_repro, level shift           |     | 0.988     | 3.26       | 2.855      |
| gen contrast_masked, level shift    |     | **1.000** | 8.72       | 5.441      |
| gen contrast_masked, Point / Global |     | 0.578     | 2.70       | 1.753      |
| gen contrast_masked, global subseq. |     | 0.329     | 0.64       | 0.326      |


The planted sizes are right: `measure_contrast` on the generated level-shift
slice gives median 0.869 against the real 0.807, and its p90 is 3.987, which
is exactly the largest real value. The mechanism is the reference set.

- The six real level-shift amplitudes are **−0.865, −0.810, −0.804, −0.781,
−0.742, +3.987** (channel-span units).
- The nominal donors sit a median of **0.033** above their channel floor and
0.841 below the ceiling.

So `sample_targets` resamples a downward step of ≈ 0.8 into five sixths of the
generated level shifts, and a window whose parent has 0.033 of downward room
cannot absorb it — the exit is arithmetic, not a tuning error. The remaining
sixth draws +3.987 and produces the 3–9 unit overshoots behind excess p95 8.7.
Real level-shift windows exit only 1 in 6 times because the real events happen
where the signal has room; nothing in the objective knows that.

This is the n = 6 reference problem of
[NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md](NEXT_STEPS_2026-09-30.md) L2/L6 in a second place:
a retrained denoiser cannot fix it, and neither can λ.

The defect is confined to the **step** target. The spike target is fine on
every variant (0.8–1.1×), and the two untargeted kinds are donor-smooth. On
the level-shift slice `flip_x0_contrast_only` regenerates the whole window at
ten times the real roughness, and `flip_x0_contrast_masked` keeps the donor
prefix exact — that part of the masking works — and returns a tail at twenty
times, with a spike at the transition reaching ±6 span units.

The mechanism follows from what the objective is. A step contrast is a
difference of means, so `contrast_edit` constrains the **mean** of the shifted
segment and says nothing about its variance; with `contrast_only` the
prototype term that would otherwise hold the texture is switched off by
construction. The sampler is free to satisfy the target with a noisy tail, and
does. The spike contrast is local (centre against flanks over ~23 bins) and
leaves no comparable freedom, which is why it survives.

So §4's reading of `flip_x0_contrast_only` as the better position fix holds
only for the position and the pooled tail statistic. On the kind it targets it
is not usable as it stands, and neither are the masked variants; C1 and
`flip_x0_final025_recur2_decay1` are the only variants whose level shifts have
the right texture, and they put the step at the start of the window.

## 8. What to do next

1. **Make the contrast target respect the parent's headroom.** Choose the sign
  per window from the donor's room (0.033 down vs 0.841 up), or reject the
   sampled size when the parent cannot absorb it, instead of resampling the
   six amplitudes blind. This is the only change that can bring the envelope
   exit fraction near the budget, and it is confined to
   `shell/contrast.py::sample_targets` plus the plan in
   `phases/fixsweep.py::_contrast_plan`.
2. **Stop reporting the sixth amplitude as if it were a distribution.** One
  +3.987 window out of six becomes one sixth of the gallery and produces
   every excursion past 4 span units.
3. **Promote the novelty pass rate to a reported metric.** It separates
  steered from unsteered galleries (0.265 → 0.903) where ARP's interval
   touches zero, and it is what explains the GenIAS result.
4. **Constrain the variance of a shifted segment, not only its mean.** §4b is
  the reason no contrast variant carried a realism claim at the time of
   writing: the step target fixes the mean of the tail and leaves its texture
   free, at 10–22× the real roughness. **Done, by a different route than the
   one guessed here — see §9.** Neither the variance contrast nor the surviving
   prototype term was needed; the roughness was a symptom of planting the shift
   on the wrong side of the window.
5. **Global subsequences remain untouched by every arm.** §8 of the checklist
  predicted this from the testbed; the ESA run confirms it at 0.15 recall
   across twelve arms. That is the generator work, not more steering terms.

## 9. Closing the realism gap on the level shift

§4b ended with the level-shift slice at 10–22× the real texture roughness and
the recommendation to constrain its variance. That diagnosis was right about
the symptom and wrong about the cause. Six measurements, each refuting the
previous fix, locate it.

### 9.1 What a real level shift looks like, and what we were making

Split each window at its best difference-of-means point and measure the mean
|first difference| inside the shifted side, separately for the segment's edges
(outer 10% each side) and its interior:


|                                                 | edge   | interior | edge / interior |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------ | -------- | --------------- |
| real level shift                                | 0.1770 | 0.0117   | 15.1            |
| `nosteer_flip_x0_masked` (donor reconstruction) | 0.0091 | 0.0099   | 0.9             |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked`                       | 0.6440 | 0.4759   | 1.4             |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room`                  | 0.3477 | 0.2031   | 1.7             |


A real level shift is **flat, jump, flat**: all of its variation is the jump.
What the generator made was a segment with the right mean and a *noisy
interior*, 17–40× the real one. The donor reconstruction is as smooth as real
(0.0099 against 0.0117), so the texture is not the denoiser's doing — the
guidance injects it. [kind_grid_best/real_level_shift.png](kind_grid_best/real_level_shift.png)
shows eight draws per recipe against all six real windows; the nearest-in-φ
galleries of §4b hide this, because they show one hand-picked draw per cell.

### 9.2 Why: the direction was inverted, and the parent could not be fixed

Every real level shift drops **from the top of the channel span to the bottom**
(pre-level 0.835–0.920, post-level 0.073–0.131, the one exception being the
ch-2 window at −3.904). The nominal donors sit at the bottom: median level
0.099, p95 0.123. So `fit_to_headroom` (§4a) was doing its job correctly and
flipping 86.5% of the sampled amplitudes upward, which is the *time reverse* of
the real morphology, into a level the channel-conditioned score has never seen.
The score then opposes the projection at every one of the 50 steps, and the
residue of that fight is the noisy interior.

Giving the slice a parent that can hold the drop does not work on this panel.
Counting nominal donors by level:


|                                                    | donors | level > 0.25 | > 0.6 | > 0.8 |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------------ | ----- | ----- |
| full nominal pool, per channel                     | 4096   | 4–11         | 1–4   | 0–4   |
| the S3 donor pool fixsweep draws from, per channel | 256    | 0–1          | 0–1   | 0–1   |


Eleven windows in the entire pool sit above 0.8, against the 64 per channel the
slice needs. The high state of these channels is not nominal behaviour at all:
it occurs only as the pre-drop state of the six labelled events. `contrast_donor_room`
(`shell/contrast.py::assign_donors_by_room`) is implemented and tested, and it
changes nothing here for exactly that reason — `flip_x0_contrast_parent` is
within noise of `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room` on every metric.

### 9.3 The fix: shift the head, not the tail

The step contrast is a difference of means, and `contrast_mask` assumes the
shifted side is the **tail** (`[p − dilate, end)`). A real level shift shifts
the **head**: it is high for the first ~0.3 h of the 4.27 h window and then
drops for good — every real split sits at 0.072 W. So `"step_head"` mirrors
the weights and the mask, and then the five positive reference amplitudes lift
a short early stretch off a low donor. That needs 0.8 of *upward* room against
the 0.9 the donor has, so nothing has to be flipped and nothing leaves the
conditional support; and the mask locks the whole tail to the donor, which is
why the tail comes out as clean as the donor reconstruction.

Two further knobs finish it. `contrast_pos` keeps the split near where the real
ones are (the frozen W/16 guard puts the reachable range at 0.0625–0.18 W).
`contrast_polish` runs an SDEdit pass (Meng et al., ICLR 2022) **on the level
shift only** — re-noise to ν = 0.35 and let the unguided denoiser walk back
down, then re-apply the projection once so the amplitude the polish removed
comes back without the chain getting another 50 steps to roughen it. Applying
it to the spike as well halves a 3-bin excursion (0.830 → 0.303), which is why
it is per-kind.

roughness, with the Point/Global spike height beside it:

| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------ | -------------------- |
| real                                                              | 1.0× (0.0160 span/bin) | 0.830        | high, drop, flat     |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked`                                         | 21.8×                  | 1.040        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room` (headroom)                         | 5.8×                   | 1.040        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_masked_room_decay2` (contrast decay)            | 5.0×                   | 1.018        | rise, noisy          |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head` (head)                                    | 5.5×                   | 1.040        | **high, drop, flat** |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head_polish3` (head + polish both kinds)        | **1.5×**               | 0.303        | high, drop, flat     |
| `flip_x0_contrast_head_kindpolish` (head + polish the shift only) | **1.5×**               | **1.040**    | **high, drop, flat** |


`contrast_decay` and `polish_reproject` are in `shell/steer.py` as opt-in

### 9.5 What is still wrong

- **The envelope exit fraction, 0.557 against the real 0.039.** The polish
pulls it down only when it runs on every kind (`head_polish3`: 0.307). The
lifted head overshoots the [0, 1] span rail by 10–20%, and the single
+3.987 reference amplitude (§4a item 2, still open) accounts for one sixth
of the slice on its own.
- **The lifted stretch is longer and more ragged than the real one**, which is
  what remains of the 1.5×; real is 1.0× by construction.
- **The level shift's position is pinned to 0.0625–0.18 W**, matching the six
  real windows, so every generated one puts its step on the left. That is
  `contrast_pos`, not the causal bias: `flip_x0_contrast_head_wide` is the same
  recipe asking for 0.0625–0.80 and achieves 0.063–0.759 (median 0.406), where
  C1 lands at 0.063–0.068 having been asked for nothing. Widening costs
  roughness, 1.5× → 2.0×. Details in
  [MODEL_CONTRAST_HEAD.md](MODEL_CONTRAST_HEAD.md).
- **Detection is not re-measured for this recipe.** The §6 table is still the
`flip_x0_contrast_only` run. `shell.augdetect.arms` needs an arm for
`flip_x0_contrast_head_kindpolish` before any detection claim is attached to
it.
